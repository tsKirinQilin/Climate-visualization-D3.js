Methodology

How the data is used

Realtime

Detailed selected-point conditions come from OpenWeather. Global temperature, cloud and wind fields come from Open-Meteo, while Realtime Rain uses the OpenWeather Weather Maps 1.0 precipitation tile layer for denser worldwide coverage.

Short-range analytics

The 24-hour and 7-day charts use Open-Meteo forecast data. They are forecasts, not observations.

Seasonal forecast

Monthly outlooks use the ECMWF SEAS5 ensemble mean through Open-Meteo. They describe broad regional tendencies rather than exact weather on a particular day.

Anomaly

A temperature anomaly is the model forecast minus the model's long-term climatology. +2 °C means warmer than that model baseline, not necessarily 2 °C warmer than last year.

Historical comparisons

Past-period comparisons used by Climate AI come from the Open-Meteo Historical Weather API and are calculated on the server.

Climate AI

Gemini interprets the question and requests application tools. Numerical weather values and comparisons are produced by the backend services; the model explains the returned data.

Seasonal precipitation

The forecast rain map shows precipitation anomaly relative to the model climatology: red means drier than the model baseline and blue means wetter. The absolute monthly mean remains available in the location panel.

Wind visualization

The wind map interpolates the sampled Open-Meteo wind-speed field into a continuous colour surface. Animated D3 streamlines use the sampled wind direction and speed to show flow.

Spatial sampling

Global temperature and wind fields use sampled geographic grids to control API load. Realtime Rain is rendered from OpenWeather raster precipitation tiles on a Web Mercator D3 map, because precipitation is too spatially local for the coarse global point grid used by temperature.

Basemap layers

The standard MAP view uses a MapTiler terrain/landscape basemap when available, while SATELLITE uses MapTiler satellite imagery. Temperature, rain and wind remain separate weather overlays above either basemap.

Freshness & limitations

Observation timestamps are shown for current weather. ECMWF SEAS5 is a regional seasonal model at roughly 36 km resolution, updates monthly around the 5th, and is not bias-corrected. It should not be treated as an exact local day-by-day forecast.
